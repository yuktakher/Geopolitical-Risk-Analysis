Executive Summary
On 27 June 2026, at around 4:30 a.m. local time, the Panama-flagged oil tanker Kiku was hit by a projectile, likely a drone (as assessed by UKMTO) in the Strait of Hormuz. CENTCOM has attributed the strike to Iran, which has not been independently verified yet. The incident happened after a similar strike on MV Ever Lovely on June 25, post which US had hit Iranian missile and drone storage locations and coastal radar sites. 

Chronology
	• June 17: MoU between Iran and US to ensure “best efforts for the safe passage of commercial vessels with no charge for 60 days”, followed by sanctions waiver after the Switzerland talks as a goodwill gesture.  
	• June 18-24: Hostilities continue despite the MoU. 
	• June 25: MV Ever Lovely struck by a suspected Iranian drone strike. 
	• June 27: Tanker Kiku struck by a projectile in Strait of Hormuz, CENTCOM attributes it to an Iranian drone. 
	• June 27 (later): US military responds back with airstrikes on Iranian military sites along the Strait.

Strategic implications:
	1. Adverse effects on the US-Iran de-escalation trajectory
	2. War risk insurance and cascading cost implications: War risk insurance is primarily underwritten at Lloyd's and set by Joint War Committee (JWC). Repeated incidents in a short time period prompt the JWC to add or expand a listed area based on risk assessment. The military authorities issue warnings and threat assessments after such incidents, signalling underwriters to increase premiums. Ship owners pay premiums and pass costs to charterers via higher freight rates. Charterers add the freight costs to the crude prices, which is passed on to the consumers by refiners. The consumers end up paying at the pump and in higher prices of goods.
